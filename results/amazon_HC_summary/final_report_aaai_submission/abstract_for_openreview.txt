Higher Criticism for Adaptive Retrieval: From Pipeline Integration to a Controlled Study of Null Calibration


---------- ABSTRACT (paste into the "Abstract" field) ----------

Data retrieval has become a load-bearing component of modern AI systems: retrieving too few passages leaves a reader to hallucinate or miss the answer, while retrieving too many wastes tokens, inflates latency, and distracts the model with irrelevant context. We view retrieval as a sparse signal detection and identification problem: across a vast corpus, only a few entries, if any, are relevant to a given query, and their similarity scores must be separated from a dense background of near-misses. We therefore propose to select the retrieved set by a method based on Higher Criticism Thresholding (HCT), a well-established selection tool from the sparse signal detection literature. We apply our HCT-based method to cutoff document relevance scores in four Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipelines. In three of them, HCT raises context precision more than tenfold over gap-based Adaptive-K on multi-hop QA and improves answer Exact Match by 23% (relative), while reading an order of magnitude fewer tokens. To see how HCT holds up in realistic deployment, where a single calibration must serve many unseen queries, we run a large-scale study on an 80,000-document product corpus. This stress-test uncovers a specific, repairable failure mode: with a naive shared calibration, HCT is over-cautious and returns nothing on more than 40% of queries, leaving the downstream reader with no context to answer from. A short, label-free correction, computed at inference time from each query's own scores, removes this failure with no extra data, no retraining, and no tuning. With the correction in place, HCT gives the best retrieval F1 among eight cutoff rules (12% above the strongest fixed Top-k), lifts end-to-end answer quality by 15% (token-F1) over a Top-20 baseline, and works from hundreds up to 100,000 documents. Notably, HCT beats even the best fixed Top-k that was hand-tuned on the same data, so a team deploying it can drop the labeled dev set and the grid search over k that a fixed-k retriever normally requires, and let each query pick its own cutoff at inference.
