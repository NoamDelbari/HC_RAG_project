Higher Criticism for Adaptive Retrieval: From Pipeline Integration to a Controlled Study of Null Calibration


---------- ABSTRACT (paste into the "Abstract" field) ----------

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) typically retrieves a fixed number of passages per query, wasting context on easy questions and under-serving hard ones. Higher Criticism (HC), a goodness-of-fit test for rare and weak signals, offers a principled alternative: cosine similarities become p-values under a calibrated null of irrelevant query–document pairs, and HC keeps only the documents whose scores are too extreme to be background noise. We evaluate HC as a drop-in cutoff in three existing RAG pipelines and in a controlled study designed to expose its failure modes. On multi-hop QA it raises context precision more than tenfold over gap-based Adaptive-K and improves answer Exact Match by 23% (relative), while reading an order of magnitude fewer tokens. Deployment, however, forces a harder setting: a real retriever must calibrate one global null offline and reuse it on every unseen query. On a controlled 80K-document product-retrieval benchmark, this shared null makes HC retrieve nothing on over 40% of queries. We trace the failure to a per-query scale mismatch between the global null and each query's non-relevant scores, and correct it with a label-free recalibration that rescales z-scores from observable pool statistics while keeping one global null. The fix recovers nearly all affected queries whose answers exist in the pool, and HC attains the best retrieval F1 of eight competing cutoff rules, 12% (relative) above the strongest fixed Top-k. The advantage carries through to end-to-end generation, improving answer token-F1 by 15% over a fixed Top-20, and holds at corpus scales up to 100,000 documents.
