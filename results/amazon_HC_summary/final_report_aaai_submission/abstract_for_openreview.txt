Higher Criticism for Adaptive Retrieval: From Pipeline Integration to a Controlled Study of Null Calibration


---------- ABSTRACT (paste into the "Abstract" field) ----------

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) typically retrieves a fixed number of passages per query, wasting context on easy questions and under-serving hard ones. Higher Criticism (HC), a goodness-of-fit test for rare and weak signals, offers a principled alternative: cosine similarities become p-values under a calibrated null of irrelevant query–document pairs, and HC keeps only the documents whose scores are too extreme to be background noise. We evaluate HC as a drop-in cutoff across three existing RAG pipelines and in a controlled study we designed to expose its failure modes. On multi-hop QA it improves answer Exact Match by 23% over gap-based Adaptive-K and raises context precision more than tenfold, while reading an order of magnitude fewer tokens. On a controlled 80K-document product-retrieval benchmark, however, a single global null—the calibration a deployable retriever must use—collapses HC to k=0 on 55 of 135 queries. We diagnose this as a per-query scale mismatch between the null and each query's non-relevant scores (r=0.83 with the failure) and correct it with a label-free recalibration that rescales z-scores from observable pool statistics while keeping one global null. The fix recovers 97% of the fixable queries and gives HC the best retrieval F1 of eight competing cutoff rules, 12% above the strongest fixed Top-k. The advantage carries to end-to-end generation—answer token-F1 improves by 15% over a fixed Top-20—and holds from 726 documents up to 100,000.
