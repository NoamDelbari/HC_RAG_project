Higher Criticism for Adaptive Retrieval: From Pipeline Integration to a Controlled Study of Null Calibration


---------- ABSTRACT (paste into the "Abstract" field) ----------

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) typically retrieves a fixed number of passages per query, wasting context on easy questions and under-serving hard ones. Higher Criticism (HC), a goodness-of-fit test for rare, weak signals, offers a principled alternative: cosine similarities become p-values under a null calibrated on irrelevant query–document pairs, and HC keeps only documents too extreme to be noise. We evaluate HC as a drop-in cutoff in three RAG pipelines and in a controlled study of its failure modes. On multi-hop QA it raises context precision more than tenfold over gap-based Adaptive-K while reading an order of magnitude fewer tokens. Deployment is harder: one global null must be calibrated offline and reused on every unseen query. On an 80K-document product-retrieval benchmark, this shared null makes HC retrieve nothing on over 40% of queries. We trace the failure to a per-query scale mismatch with the null and correct it with a label-free recalibration that rescales z-scores from observable pool statistics. The fix recovers nearly all answerable failures; HC then attains the best retrieval F1 of eight cutoff rules, 12% (relative) above the strongest fixed Top-k, and improves end-to-end answer token-F1 by 15% over a fixed Top-20 at scales up to 100,000 documents.
